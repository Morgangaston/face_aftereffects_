## 💡 Summary & Core Argument
*A 2–3 sentence high-level overview of the work's central thesis or primary contribution.*
Tested several face aftereffect paradigms included the influence of color, size, and gender. They also found a week effect or order where earlier trials were more accurate. Authors are unsure of the cause of this effect. They consider low level adaptation but opt for higher order explanations. The authors found that face shapes contributed strongly to aftereffects whereas color, size and gender did not.

### 🟡 Key Points

> "the aftereffect must be selective for computationally opposite identities and not be a generalized contrast effect" (p. 2)
  * Comment: defined by a mathematical model rather than vibes

> "there is something special about the position of the average face and the axis between it and a given face." (p. 2)


> "we predicted that exposure to antifaces would result in an aftereffect such that a neutral face would resemble the real identity, leading to correct identification." (p. 3)


> "Recognition rates across experiments 1A and 1B in the post-test were very high with an average of 94%" (p. 4)


> "Identity was recognized at rates greater than chance in both experiments." (p. 4)


> "Additional tests demonstrated that there was a weak effect of order such that accuracy was higher for earlier than later trials." (p. 4)


> "it is possible that perception of correct identity after exposure to antifaces could be explained by several low-level aftereffects for orientation, spatial frequency, and color" (p. 4)


> "such low-level adaptation appears unlikely" (p. 4)


> "Identity was recognized at rates greater than chance for shape adaptation in experiment 3A but not for color adaptation" (p. 5)


> "Experiments 3A and 3B demonstrated that shape rather than color is mainly responsible for accurate identification after exposure to antifaces." (p. 6)


> "Experiment 4 demonstrated that transformations through an androgynous prototype, creating a male adapting face with the opposite shape and color characteristics, resulted in no accurate aftereffects for identity." (p. 7)


> "We found that shape information was of greater importance than color in coding face identity" (p. 8)


> "We note that although the results point to the locus of adaptation being mechanisms for coding high-level aspects of faces, further work is also needed to fully rule out lower level explanations/loci." (p. 8)
